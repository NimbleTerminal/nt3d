# Demos

The demos are complete programs in [`examples/demos`](https://github.com/NimbleTerminal/nt3d/tree/main/examples/demos), and the quickest way to see what nt3d does. Each runs in a terminal with `task <name>`, and in a browser from the gallery.

They all share the widget's [controls](../widget/#controls) (drag to orbit, wheel to zoom, `c` to fit, `r` to rotate) and a few keys of their own: `1`, `2`, and `3` switch between GPU, software, and wireframe, and `q` quits. Each demo lists its other keys along the top of the screen.

## Terrain explorer

<img class="poster" src="../../posters/terrain.png" alt="Textured rolling terrain with a small red marker">

`task terrain`. A textured [heightfield](../shapes/#terrain) with lighting, picking, and parent transforms. `f` flattens the hills, `t` swaps the texture, `l` toggles lighting, `n` rotates the parent node, `v` hides it, and `p` switches to shader textures.

Its flags work for every demo: `--render-mode gpu|software|wireframe`, `--snapshot file.png` for a still image, `--duration`, `--animate=false` to start paused, and `--medium direct|shm` to choose how Kitty images are sent.

```sh
task terrain -- --render-mode software
task terrain -- --render-mode gpu --snapshot /tmp/terrain.png
```

## Procedural plasma

<img class="poster" src="../../posters/procedural.png" alt="Rainbow plasma bands generated by a shader and mapped onto terrain">

`task procedural`. A [shader texture](../materials/#shader-textures) paints the terrain with animated plasma, up to 30 times a second, skipping updates while the widget is busy. `a` pauses it, `t` shifts its phase, and software rendering shows the CPU fallback. Snapshots capture the first phase.

```sh
task terrain -- --procedural --snapshot /tmp/plasma-gpu.png
task terrain -- --procedural --render-mode software --snapshot /tmp/plasma-software.png
```

## Dynamic scene

<img class="poster" src="../../posters/dynamic.png" alt="Colorful boxes orbit a teal wave above a textured floor">

`task dynamic`. Boxes orbit a deforming wave above a textured floor. The wave changes its vertices and even its triangles each step; the boxes only change their transforms. `m` holds the wave so you can watch transform-only animation, `b` adds a box, `x` removes the oldest, `a` pauses, and `v` toggles visibility.

## Shape builders

<img class="poster" src="../../posters/shapes.png" alt="A textured cube, golden torus, purple cone, orange cylinder, and green globe in a row">

`task shapes`. Every [shape builder](../shapes/), lit and textured. `s` cycles the normals between as built, flat, and smooth; `t` turns them to glass, `e` adds highlights, `b` toggles [silhouette outlines](../materials/#outlines), and `d` adds fog.

## Mesh files

<img class="poster" src="../../posters/models.png" alt="A striped robot with a sky-blue head, orange arms, and a smiling screen">

`task models`. Three sample files: a 3MF robot (components with a mirrored arm, a corner-color gradient, slicer-painted stripes, a textured screen, and a thumbnail), a Y-up OBJ star (concave caps, a wood texture, an MTL color), and a binary STL trefoil knot with face colors. `[` and `]` change model, and `s` cycles normals. The samples are made from nt3d's own shape builders by `task models-generate`, so no third-party models are included.

`task meshes -- model.stl` shows your own files, any format nt3d reads; add `--up y` for Y-up OBJ files.

## Dense mesh

<img class="poster" src="../../posters/large.png" alt="A lumpy gray asteroid lit from one side">

`task large`. A 735,000-triangle asteroid. Software draws every triangle, wireframe draws its silhouette and contours, and clicks [pick](../picking/) triangles through an index built in the background; `i` turns indexing off.

## Fur

<img class="poster" src="../../posters/fur.png" alt="Spot the cow covered in black-and-white fur">

`task fur`. Spot the cow in [shell fur](../fur/) colored by its own texture, swaying in the wind by moving nodes alone. `s` cycles the shell count, so you can watch layers become strands; `l` changes the length, `f` toggles the [fins](../fur/#fins), `a` the wind, `x` supersamples, and `e` cycles post effects.

## Mipmaps

<img class="poster" src="../../posters/mipmaps.png" alt="A checkered floor running to the horizon, fading to an even gray, with an orange sphere">

`task mipmaps`. A tiled floor to the horizon. `m` cycles the [filtering](../materials/#mipmaps): without mipmaps the far texels shimmer into moiré; with them the floor settles into an even tone, and anisotropic filtering keeps its detail. `t` changes the texture, `x` supersamples, and `d` adds fog.

## Forest

<img class="poster" src="../../posters/forest.png" alt="A forest of pines and round trees on green hills, fading into fog">

`task forest`. Thousands of trees from three [shared meshes](../scenes/#sharing-geometry), each uploaded once and drawn in one instanced call, swaying in the wind. `i` switches them to separate copies to compare, `n` changes how many trees grow, `a` stills the wind, `d` fades them into fog, and `s` cycles through no shadows, drop shadows, and shadow maps.

## Lights

<img class="poster" src="../../posters/lights.png" alt="A sphere, torus, column, and cube on a floor lit by eight colored point lights in a ring">

`task lights`. A sphere, a torus, a column, and a cube on a floor of two triangles, under presets of [lighting](../rendering/#how-it-looks): the default key light, a turning sun under a sky-to-ground ambient, three colored lamps orbiting, one lamp at night, and eight at once. Light is found per pixel, so a lamp's bright spot crosses the floor where no vertex is. `l` changes the preset, `f` the point lights' falloff, `e` turns highlights off, `a` pauses the lamps, and `s` cycles through no shadows, drop shadows, and shadow maps. Shadow maps need a directional light, so the point-light presets cast none.

The first three presets demonstrate [environment skies and diffuse image-based lighting](../environment/): a gradient sky with a key light, the sky alone, and an original generated warm/cool panorama. `l` cycles through them and the direct-light presets. Sky orientation follows the camera in all three renderers. Press `n` to compare the generated relief normal map with smooth surfaces; both GPU and software perturb the lighting, while wireframe keeps the geometric silhouette.

## Particles

<img class="poster" src="../../posters/particles.png" alt="A fire of glowing points rising into gray smoke over a dark floor">

`task particles`. A fire of soft points sized in world units, glowing [additively](../rendering/#how-it-looks), rises into smoke blended by alpha, sorted back to front every frame, while dust drifts through in pixel-sized points. Every point moves every frame through [`SetNodePoints`](../particles/), keeping its GPU buffers and using fixed bounds. `t` switches to a generated four-frame sprite atlas. `b` changes the blending, `s` makes the points hard or soft, `n` changes the count (1,000, 10,000, or 100,000), and `a` pauses them.

## In a browser

The [gallery](https://nimbleterminal.github.io/nt3d/) is online. To build and serve it yourself:

```sh
task build-web  # or task build-site, to include this guide at /docs/
task serve      # then open http://localhost:8768
```

The gallery links to every demo, each running inside [go-booba](https://github.com/NimbleMarkets/go-booba) with GPU, software, and wireframe rendering. The site's sources are in `web/`, and the built site in `dist/web/` can be served from any subdirectory.

## Animation

<img class="poster" src="../../posters/animation.png" alt="A ribbon bent by animated joints and a morph target">

`task animation`. An original generated CC0 glTF ribbon bends on two joints, changes shape through a morph target, and plays translation, quaternion rotation, and scale tracks. `a` pauses; `1`, `2`, and `3` switch between GPU, software, and wireframe. The demo loads through glTF and drives the [generic animation API](../animation/).

## glTF animation lab

<img class="poster" src="../../posters/animate.png" alt="An articulated assembly with textured tiles">

`task animate` opens an original generated assembly, followed by three self-contained [Khronos glTF samples](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF-Sample-Assets):

- **Assembly lab** (6 KB GLB): nested turntable, shoulder, and tool pivots; authored perspective and orthographic cameras; reference and transformed texture tiles. The tool combines transformed color UVs with independent relief normal-map UVs. Press `k` to cycle the orbit and authored cameras. Camera navigation restores the saved orbit view. GPU and software show the textures; wireframe shows the articulated structure.

- **AnimatedMorphCube** (GLB): two morph targets turn a cube into a thin slab and a ramp.
- **SimpleSkin** (glTF plus binary buffers): two joints bend a strip through blended vertex weights and inverse bind matrices.
- **AnimatedTriangle** (glTF plus binary buffers): quaternion keyframes rotate a triangle.

`[` and `]` change models, `a` toggles play/pause, Enter restarts, and `,` and `.` switch clips when a model has more than one. The bundled samples each have one clip. `1`, `2`, and `3` select GPU, software, and wireframe. Playback uses the generic animation API, and changing clips starts from a fresh instance so untracked channels return to the bind pose.

The original assembly is **CC0** and can be regenerated with `go -C examples/demos generate ./internal/demo/gltf`. The Khronos model assets are **CC0**, by Microsoft (AnimatedMorphCube) and Marco Hutter (SimpleSkin and AnimatedTriangle). Upstream metadata is **CC-BY-4.0**. Original READMEs and licenses accompany every sample, with a pinned source revision in `examples/demos/internal/demo/gltf/SOURCES.md`. All sample files together, including provenance, occupy less than 30 KB.

Only `cmd/animate` imports the glTF sample embed package and assigns its `Files` to `demo.SampleFiles`. Relative `.bin` files are read through the mesh loader's `Options.Open`. Other demo binaries do not embed these samples, and the separate `examples/demos` module keeps them out of the main library's distribution. The generated `animation` demo and general-purpose `meshes` viewer and Gobkit character demo also opt into glTF decoding; other demos do not import that decoder.

## Gobkit characters

<img class="poster" src="../../posters/characters.png" alt="An animated Gobkit minion">

Press `b` for dark silhouette outlines in either GPU or software. The setting
survives character and clip changes, and toggling it keeps the current pose.

`task characters` plays **Minion A-1**, **Corgi**, and **Duck** from [Gobkit](https://gobkit.com).
All three have named `idle`, `attack`, and `dead` clips; the animals also have `walk`.
`[` / `]` change characters, `,` / `.` change clips, `a` toggles playback, and
**Enter** restarts the current clip (remaining paused if paused). Clips loop.
`1` / `2` / `3` select GPU, software, and wireframe. The status line names the
character, clip, and source. The existing `task animate` glTF gallery remains available.

The original GLBs total **399,144 bytes** and are **CC0 1.0** (public domain,
no attribution required). License, download URLs, and SHA-256 checksums accompany
the assets in `examples/demos/internal/demo/gobkit`. Only `cmd/characters` imports
that embed package and supplies `demo.SampleFiles`; the files stay in the separate
demos module and are absent from library builds and other demo binaries.
Playback uses the same generic animation API and sample player as the Khronos gallery.
